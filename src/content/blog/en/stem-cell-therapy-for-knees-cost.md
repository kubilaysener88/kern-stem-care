## Short answer

Stem cell therapy for knees costs **$2,300 USD for one knee** and **$4,250 USD for both** through the
partner clinics we coordinate with in Cancún. That is 56 million cells per knee, in one application, and the
figure covers the medical evaluation, the cells, the application at the clinic and our coordination. Travel
is separate.

Elsewhere you will see very different numbers. In the United States, a study published in 2018 asked stem cell
centers for a quote: the 65 that gave one averaged $5,156 USD per knee. For Mexico, one medical-travel portal
listed $2,000 to $4,000 USD per knee in November 2026.

Before comparing prices, it helps to know what you are buying: the injection may ease pain slightly, but it has not
been shown to regrow cartilage, and in one large trial it was no better than a cortisone shot at one year.
These therapies are considered investigational and are not approved by the U.S. FDA or Health Canada for knee
osteoarthritis. Results vary from person to person.

## More cells is no guarantee of a better result

The cell count is an easy way to justify a high price: "200 million" sounds twice as good as "100 million."
The knee studies do not support that arithmetic.

- **Six randomised trials, 300 people.** A 2025 meta-analysis pooled single injections of between 16 and 100
  million cells. At 25 million or fewer there was already a significant improvement at 12 months, and higher
  doses gave no significant additional benefit. The authors caution that the analysis is exploratory, with
  few studies behind it.
- **Three doses of umbilical-cord cells, in Chile.** A phase I trial published in 2024, with no control group,
  tested 2, 20 and 80 million. All three groups improved from where they started at 3 and 6 months, the low and medium doses more
  than the high one. At 80 million, *every* patient had injection-related swelling in the knee. There were no
  serious adverse events at any dose.
- **And one review that points the other way.** Another from 2025, covering eight trials of fat-derived cells,
  ranked the high doses above the low donor-cell dose for both pain and function.

In short, the best dose is not known. That applies to us too. The 56 million in our protocol is within the
range that has been studied and above that 25, with no proof that it works better than a smaller number. The treating physician confirms the number for your case in writing, and it is fair to ask why they
recommend it.

When you compare offers, ask for the count *per knee*, how many of the cells are alive when they are injected
(viability), and which tissue they come from. Our piece on
[what a cell therapy center does](/en/blog/what-is-a-cell-therapy-center/) explains how to read the batch
certificate.

## Is it worth the money? What the evidence says

- **Against a placebo, a small benefit.** A 2025 Cochrane review found that, in seven trials with 445 people,
  pain improved by about 1.2 points on a 0-to-10 scale, up to six months out. The certainty of that evidence
  is low. The same review notes that any injection into a joint carries a small risk of infection.
- **Against cortisone, no advantage at one year.** The MILES trial in the United States randomly assigned 480
  people with knee osteoarthritis to one of three kinds of cells — bone marrow, fat or umbilical cord — or to a
  cortisone injection. At 12 months all four groups had less pain: about 20 points less on a 100-point scale
  with umbilical-cord cells, and about 21 with cortisone. None was superior to another, and the MRI did not
  change significantly in any of them. Knee swelling was more common with umbilical-cord cells (24%) than with
  cortisone (7%). There were no procedure-related serious adverse events. Those are the
  products used in that trial, given as a single injection; they do not by themselves describe what any given
  clinic prepares.
- **Who responded.** A later analysis of the same trial found, on one of its two pain scales, more responders
  with bone-marrow cells than with cortisone, and differences between men and women. It is a lead for future studies, not yet a rule for
  choosing a treatment.

A cortisone shot costs less, and you can have it near home. If you have never tried one, ask your doctor
whether it makes sense before you travel. And if you had one recently, say so at your evaluation: one knee
trial required 12 weeks without it, as we explain in
[who is a candidate](/en/blog/who-is-a-candidate-for-stem-cell-therapy/).

## Five questions for any knee quote

1. **Is the price for one knee or both?** Several of the listings we checked did not say.
2. **How many cells does each knee receive, and from which tissue?**
3. **How many applications are included?** In a 2026 trial in China, 25 people with advanced osteoarthritis
   received four injections, and a year later had less pain than another 25 who received hyaluronic acid. It
   is a small, single-center study, and four injections do not cost the same as one.
4. **Is the injection ultrasound-guided?** In our network it depends on the area; ask which applies to your
   case.
5. **Which physician reviewed my studies, and what happens if they say no?** If the price arrives before the
   review, a step is missing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Medicare or insurance pay for stem cell knee treatment?
As a rule, no: plan to pay out of pocket. The details by insurer are in
[does insurance cover stem cell therapy?](/en/blog/does-insurance-cover-stem-cell-therapy/)

### Why is it cheaper in Mexico than in the U.S.?
Regulation, overhead and the product itself all differ, so a lower price on its own is neither a warning nor
a bargain. What to check is in
[Cancún vs. Tijuana vs. the U.S.](/en/blog/stem-cell-therapy-cost-cancun-tijuana-usa/)

### Do stem cells regrow knee cartilage?
There is no good evidence that an injection rebuilds a worn knee. We go through the studies in
[can stem cells repair knee cartilage?](/en/blog/can-stem-cells-repair-knee-cartilage/)

### Can you walk after a stem cell knee injection?
Plan to rest for the remainder of treatment day, and expect some soreness or swelling for the first several
days. Ask your treating physician when you can load the knee normally, climb stairs and exercise.
[What a normal flare looks like](/en/blog/pain-worse-before-better-stem-cell-therapy/) covers what to expect
and when to call.

### How long does a stem cell knee injection last?
Most of the solid data is short-term: the Cochrane review's main results run to six months. One 30-patient
study followed people for four years. See
[how long stem cell therapy lasts](/en/blog/how-long-does-stem-cell-therapy-last/).

### When do I pay?
Once a physician has reviewed your case on a video consultation and confirmed you are a candidate: one
payment, by 4pm the day before the application. The evaluation is free, and if you are not a candidate it
ends there.
