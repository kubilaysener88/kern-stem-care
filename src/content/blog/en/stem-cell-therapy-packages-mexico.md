## Short answer

Stem cell therapy packages in Mexico are commonly listed from about **$4,000 to $15,000 USD**:
that is where most packages sat on the first page of one large medical-tourism marketplace in
November 2026.
The headline figure alone tells you very little, because every package puts a different mix
inside it — some include a hotel and airport transfers, others only the cells and the
application, and the cell count and number of applications vary too. Two packages at the same
price can be very different purchases.

The way to compare them is to take each package apart into the same lines: the medical
evaluation, the cells (how many, what kind, how they are given), the number of applications,
and then the travel. Once both quotes are written that way, the cheaper one is usually obvious.

Regenerative cell therapies are considered investigational and are not approved by the U.S. FDA
or Health Canada for these uses. Results vary from person to person.

## What a package usually bundles

A package is a single price for a set of items. The items fall into two groups, and it helps to
keep them apart from the start.

**The medical side** — the consultation and evaluation, any lab tests or imaging, the cell
product and its laboratory preparation, the application itself, and monitoring afterwards.

**The trip** — hotel nights, airport transfers, sometimes meals, and occasionally extras such
as a vitamin drip or an "exosome booster" added to the cell treatment.

The word "all-inclusive" does not settle which of these you are getting. One package we read,
advertised as all-inclusive, listed hotel accommodation and daily meals under "What is not
included." That is not dishonest — it says so in the fine print — but it shows why the list
matters more than the label.

## Six questions that turn a package into a comparable price

Ask each clinic these, and ask for the answers in writing.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **How many cells, and of what type?** | "Up to" a number is not a number. Ask for the count you will receive and whether it is per joint or in total. |
| **How are they given?** | An injection into a joint and an IV infusion are different treatments — [here is how they differ](/en/blog/stem-cell-iv-vs-joint-injection/). A package may combine both. |
| **How many applications?** | One session and a staged protocol over several visits are not the same price, even when the package costs the same. |
| **Which tests are included?** | Some packages include lab work and imaging; others expect you to bring them or charge separately. |
| **How many hotel nights, and which hotel?** | A night you would have booked anyway has value. A night in a hotel you would never have chosen has less. |
| **What happens if the physician says no?** | Find out whether any deposit is refundable, and at what point. A package paid before anyone has reviewed your studies leaves this question open. |

Write the answers side by side and subtract the travel lines. What remains is the price of the
treatment, and that is the number to compare.

## Signs to slow down

Most packages are sold in good faith. These are the patterns worth a second look before you pay:

- **A fixed price for a serious disease before a physician has seen your history.** The
  protocol for a complex condition should come out of an evaluation, not a price list.
- **A cell count with "up to" in front of it,** or no count at all.
- **No laboratory named.** You should be able to find out which laboratory prepares the cells
  and check that it is government-licensed (COFEPRIS). Our [guide to choosing a clinic](/en/how-to-choose-a-clinic/)
  covers how.
- **A discount that expires this week.** Prices do change, but treatment decisions made under a
  deadline tend to be worse ones.
- **Extras that pad the package.** A bonus drip or a free night is fine. It is not a reason to
  choose one treatment over another.

## Payment plans, deposits and monthly payments

Many patients search for a payment plan. If a clinic offers monthly payments or financing through
a third party, ask for the total you will pay with interest included, and
compare that figure — not the monthly one — with the cash price. No one can promise how much a
treatment will help, so it is worth being sure about the cost before you borrow for it.

Ask, too, where a deposit goes and when it becomes non-refundable. The safest order is the one
in which you pay nothing until a physician has reviewed your case and said you are a candidate.

## FAQ

### How much is a stem cell therapy package in Mexico?
Listed packages commonly run from about $4,000 to $15,000 USD, and some go higher for serious
conditions. The range is wide because the contents differ: cell count, number of applications,
tests and travel are all bundled differently. For per-application figures, see
[how much stem cell therapy costs in Mexico](/en/blog/how-much-does-stem-cell-therapy-cost-in-mexico/).

### Is an all-inclusive package cheaper than paying for each part?
Sometimes. A package can be good value if you would have used everything in it. If it includes
a hotel you would not have chosen or a second treatment you did not need, an itemized quote can
come out lower. Compare the medical lines on their own first.

### Do packages include the flight?
Usually not. Most packages leave out airfare, and many leave out meals and the hotel as well. Check
the "not included" list, not just the headline.

### Can I pay for stem cell therapy in monthly payments?
If a clinic offers financing, compare the total with interest, not the monthly figure, against
the cash price. We do not offer instalment plans; you pay in
full the day before your application, and only after a physician has confirmed you are a
candidate.

### Should I pay a deposit to reserve a package?
Only if you know in writing when it becomes non-refundable, and ideally only after a physician
has reviewed your case. With us there is no deposit at any stage.
