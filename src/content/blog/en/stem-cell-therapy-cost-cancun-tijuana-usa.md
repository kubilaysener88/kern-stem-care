## Short answer

Search around and you'll find wildly different numbers for **[stem cell therapy](/en/services/stem-cells/)**
depending on whether the quote says Cancún, Tijuana, or a U.S. clinic. The gap isn't really about the city on
the map — it comes down to four things: the lab's licensing and cell-sourcing standards, the treating
physician's experience, how complex the protocol is, and each country's regulatory environment, which shapes
what can legally be offered at all. A lower number because a lab skipped one of those isn't a discount. See
what we publish for our own Cancún network in [our full cost breakdown](/en/blog/how-much-does-stem-cell-therapy-cost-in-mexico/).

## Why U.S. pricing looks so different

In the United States, most cellular therapies for orthopedic, wellness, or anti-aging use are not approved by
the FDA for those specific conditions — see our guide on [what "investigational" means](/en/blog/is-stem-cell-therapy-legal-in-mexico/).
That regulatory status shapes both availability and price: clinics offering them domestically often carry
higher facility, malpractice-insurance, and compliance overhead than an equivalent procedure would in a
country with a different regulatory framework for the same investigational therapies. This is a structural
market difference, not a statement about which country's medicine is "better" — it's part of why patients
research options outside the U.S. in the first place.

## Why costs vary within Mexico too — it isn't about the city name

Mexico isn't a single price. A quote from Tijuana and a quote from Cancún can differ from each other for the
same reasons a U.S. quote differs from either: the specific lab's licensing status, the cell source and
preparation standards, the physician's experience, and how many sessions the protocol requires. **The city a
clinic is located in tells you nothing on its own about whether it's properly licensed** — that has to be
verified clinic by clinic, everywhere, including in Cancún. A 2022 literature review on international stem
cell tourism singles out lack of independent verification — not any one destination — as the core risk factor
for patients traveling for these therapies ([Lyons et al., 2022, *International Health*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8890798/) — peer-reviewed, National Institutes of Health).

| What drives the price | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Lab licensing & cell-sourcing standards | A government-licensed (COFEPRIS) lab absorbs costs — batch testing, traceability, sterility controls — that an unlicensed one simply skips |
| Physician experience | More experienced physicians can charge more for the same protocol |
| Protocol complexity | A single session costs less than a multi-stage protocol built for a complex case |
| Country's regulatory framework | Shapes what's legally offered, under what oversight, and at what facility cost |

## What to check, regardless of which city you're comparing

- Does the lab hold a current government license (COFEPRIS in Mexico), verifiable independently — not just claimed on a website?
- Is the treating physician's *cédula profesional* (or equivalent licence) current and checkable?
- Does the quote come itemized in writing, before you book travel — see our [full cost breakdown](/en/blog/how-much-does-stem-cell-therapy-cost-in-mexico/) for what a detailed Cancún quote looks like?
- What does the clinic's safety and vetting process actually look like? Our [safety guide](/en/blog/is-stem-cell-therapy-safe/) and
  [how to choose a clinic](/en/how-to-choose-a-clinic/) checklist cover exactly this.

A lower price that skips independent verification on any of these isn't a bargain — it's a different (and
riskier) product than a licensed one, no matter which city it's quoted from.

## Common questions

### Is stem cell therapy cheaper in Mexico than in the U.S.?
Quotes are often lower, largely because the regulatory and facility-overhead structure is different for
therapies that are investigational in the U.S. But "cheaper" and "properly licensed" are two separate
questions — verify the second one regardless of the number.

### Is Tijuana or Cancún the safer choice?
Neither city name answers that question on its own. Government licensing (COFEPRIS), a verifiable *cédula
profesional* for the treating physician, and a written, itemized quote matter in any Mexican city — including
ours. Judge the specific clinic and lab, not the destination.

### Why do U.S. clinics charge more for the same therapy?
Largely regulatory: because most of these therapies are not FDA-approved for specific conditions, U.S.
providers offering them domestically often carry compliance, facility, and malpractice-insurance costs that
differ from clinics operating under Mexico's regulatory framework for the same investigational therapies.

### Are these therapies approved anywhere?
No cellular therapy we coordinate is approved by the U.S. FDA or Health Canada for specific diseases; all are
considered investigational. See our [legality and regulation guide](/en/blog/is-stem-cell-therapy-legal-in-mexico/)
for how Mexico's COFEPRIS framework applies.

### How do I know if a clinic quote is legitimate, wherever it's from?
Ask for the lab's current government license, the physician's licence number, and an itemized quote in
writing before you travel. If a provider can't produce those on request, that's the red flag — not the city.
