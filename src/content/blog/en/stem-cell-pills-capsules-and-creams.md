## Short answer

Stem cell pills, capsules and creams do not contain living human stem cells: going by their
ingredients, what they usually hold is an extract of plants, fruit or algae. In online stores in
Mexico we saw bottles listed at roughly 875 to 1,400 Mexican pesos (November 2026). They may
do other things, but they are not the same as a stem cell treatment given by a physician, and the
evidence that they "activate" your own stem cells is very thin.

If you landed here comparing prices, the real difference is not the price. It is what is in the
bottle.

## What is actually inside each product

The name is the same; what sits behind it is not:

| Product | What it usually contains | Living human stem cells? |
|---|---|---|
| "Stem cell" pills or capsules | Extracts of algae (such as *Aphanizomenon flos-aquae*), plants, fruit, vitamins | No |
| Stem cell "activators" or "enhancers" | Blends of plant extracts that claim to stimulate your own cells | No |
| Creams and serums with "stem cells" | Extracts of cultured plant cells (apple, grape, argan…) | No |
| Mesenchymal stem cell treatment | Living human cells, prepared in a licensed laboratory and given by a physician | Yes |

An apple cell cannot become human skin. What the cream delivers is an extract — antioxidants and
other plant molecules — not a cell that joins your tissue.

## What the evidence says

**On the creams.** A 2017 review by Rutgers researchers of cosmetics made with plant stem cells
sets out the challenges still to overcome before these products produce meaningful changes in human skin
([Trehan et al., *Future Science OA*](https://doi.org/10.4155/fsoa-2017-0026)). The interest is
real, but proof of a clear benefit in people is still limited.

**On the capsules that "mobilize" stem cells.** One of the few studies in people is a 2007
trial in **12 healthy adults**: after taking an extract of the alga *Aphanizomenon flos-aquae*,
the number of CD34+ cells in the blood rose by 18% on average, peaking at one hour and then
fading ([Jensen et al., *Cardiovascular Revascularization Medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.carrev.2007.03.004)).
CD34+ cells are blood-forming (hematopoietic) stem cells, not the mesenchymal cells used in
joints. The study measured a blood count over a few hours; it did not measure whether anyone had
less pain, better skin or more energy.

A small study does not prove a product does nothing. It shows how far the label's promise runs
beyond what has been measured.

## What Mexican law says

Mexico's General Health Law defines **dietary supplements** as products made from herbs, plant
extracts, traditional foods or fruit concentrates whose intended use is to increase total dietary
intake, complement it or replace one of its components (Article 215, section V). Legally, they are food supplements, not medicines.

Article 414 Bis lets the health authority seize supplements or cosmetics that are promoted as
medicines, or credited with therapeutic effects, without being registered as such. If a bottle
promises to "regenerate cartilage" or "cure" something, its legal category does not back that
promise.

Human cells used in a treatment, by contrast, are prepared in government-licensed (COFEPRIS)
laboratories and given by a physician. We explain how that licensing works in
[Is stem cell therapy legal in Mexico?](/en/blog/is-stem-cell-therapy-legal-in-mexico/).

## FAQ

### Are stem cell pills harmful?
That depends on what they contain, not on the name. As supplements, they do not go through the
trials a medicine does. If you take medication, are pregnant or have a chronic condition, ask
your doctor before taking them, and be wary of any bottle that does not list its ingredients
clearly.

### Do apple stem cell creams make skin look younger?
They can moisturize and supply antioxidants, like other creams. What they do not do is put living
stem cells into your skin. The scientific review of these cosmetics points to challenges still to
overcome before they produce meaningful changes in human skin.

### Is an online "stem cell kit" the same as a treatment?
No. A treatment with human stem cells is not sold by mail: a licensed laboratory prepares it and
a physician gives it after evaluating you. If a kit offers human cells for you to apply at home,
that is a reason not to buy it.

### Can I take supplements before or after a treatment?
Tell the treating physician during your evaluation. They decide what to pause or continue for
your case.
