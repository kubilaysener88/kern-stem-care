## Short answer

Mexico's regenerative cell therapy market is mostly private and paid out of pocket: the official
list kept by Mexico's federal health authority, COFEPRIS, has **52 entries**, **20 in Baja
California** — almost all in Tijuana — 14 in Jalisco and 6 in Quintana Roo. A stem cell application in one joint
costs **$2,300 USD** in what we coordinate in Cancún.

It is the only official count we found, and it has a serious limit: its newest entry is from
2018. It shows how the market is spread out, not how many clinics operate today. This page explains
what is on it, who takes part in the market, who regulates it, and what all of that means when you
are comparing options.

## First: what is sold under the name "cell therapy"

Two very different markets share the name:

- **Bone marrow transplant.** Uses *hematopoietic* (blood-forming) stem cells to treat blood
  cancers. It lives in hospitals, with chemotherapy and an inpatient stay.
- **Regenerative medicine.** Mostly uses *mesenchymal* stem cells (MSCs) for joints, orthopedics,
  skin and wellness. It is outpatient and almost always private.

When a patient from the U.S. or Canada talks about "cell therapy in Mexico", they nearly always
mean the second. It is what we coordinate, and what this page is about. We cover the difference in
more detail in [what is a cell therapy center](/en/blog/what-is-a-cell-therapy-center/).

## Where it is concentrated: what COFEPRIS's list shows

COFEPRIS publishes a [*list of establishments that use stem
cells and regenerative medicine*](https://www.gob.mx/cms/uploads/attachment/file/411722/SEASS_REGEN.pdf).
We downloaded it on October 29, 2026 and counted every entry by the state code in its licence
number (how to read that number is in [is stem cell therapy legal in
Mexico?](/en/blog/is-stem-cell-therapy-legal-in-mexico/)).

| State | Entries |
| --- | --- |
| Baja California (17 in Tijuana) | 20 |
| Jalisco | 14 |
| Quintana Roo | 6 |
| Mexico City | 4 |
| Chihuahua | 2 |
| Nuevo León | 2 |
| Baja California Sur, State of Mexico, Morelos and Tabasco | 1 each |
| **Total** | **52** |

Three things to know when you read that table:

1. **An entry is not a clinic.** Some establishments appear two or three times, once for each
   activity they are authorised for. That is why we say entries, not clinics.
2. **It stops in 2018.** The licences on the list were issued between 2015 and 2018. Anything
   licensed later is missing, so absence does not mean unlicensed.
3. **The spread does say something.** More than half the entries sit in two states, and Tijuana
   alone has more entries than any other whole state.

## What activities it authorises, and why that matters

The list also says *what* each licence is for. Of the 52 entries:

| Authorised activity | Entries | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regenerative medicine | 26 | Applying regenerative treatments to patients |
| Cell bank (including handling for transplant) | 18 | Storing and safeguarding cells |
| Collection center | 8 | Obtaining the cells from the donor or patient |

The practical upshot: the clinic that applies your treatment and the laboratory that prepares the
cells are separate establishments with separate licences. "We are licensed by COFEPRIS" does not
say which one, or for which address. Ask for the licence that covers what will be done to you.

## Who takes part in the market

Behind a treatment there are usually four parties, each with its own responsibility:

- **The laboratory.** Sources, processes, expands and tests the cells. It needs its own health
  licence. In our network that is NAO Biotechnology, Licencia Sanitaria No. 2414150080080416.
- **The clinic.** The place where the treatment is applied, with its own government licence
  (COFEPRIS).
- **The treating physician.** Decides whether you are a candidate and applies the cells. Their
  *cédula profesional* (professional licence) can be looked up in Mexico's public registry.
- **The coordinator or agency.** That is Kern Stem Care's role: we verify the other three,
  organise the trip and stay with you throughout. Medical care is provided directly by the
  independent, licensed provider.

The licences and *cédulas* of our network are published under [what you can
verify](/en/about/#our-network).

## Why patients come from the U.S. and Canada

There are two underlying reasons, and neither is that the treatment is better proven here:

- **What each country allows.** In the U.S., without FDA approval a clinic may generally offer only
  minimally processed cells, for a narrow set of uses; cells grown in a lab to larger numbers
  ("expanded") need approval or a clinical trial. In Mexico, licensed laboratories may prepare them.
- **Cost and coverage.** Insurers usually decline these therapies as investigational, so patients
  compare prices they pay themselves. We go through it in [does insurance cover stem cell
  therapy?](/en/blog/does-insurance-cover-stem-cell-therapy/)

What does not change at the border: many of these therapies **are considered investigational and
are not approved by the U.S. FDA or Health Canada** for specific conditions. A COFEPRIS licence
authorises the establishment; it does not prove a treatment works for your condition.

## What nobody can tell you precisely

**The size of the Mexican market in dollars.** There is no official, public figure. The ones that
come up when you search for it come from market-research firms and, in what we reviewed, describe
the global market, not Mexico's. Be wary of any clinic that cites "a multi-billion-dollar market"
as a sign its treatment works: the size of a market says nothing about the evidence.

**How many clinics operate today.** There is no public, up-to-date search for establishment
licences. For a specific provider, the way to know is still to ask for the document and check that
the name and address match. The steps are in [how to choose a stem cell clinic in
Mexico](/en/how-to-choose-a-clinic/).

## FAQ

### How many stem cell clinics are there in Mexico?
There is no official, current number. COFEPRIS's list has 52 entries with licences issued between
2015 and 2018, and some establishments appear more than once. Anything authorised later is not
included.

### Why are there so many clinics in Tijuana?
The list gives locations, not reasons: 17 of the 52 entries are in Tijuana. Its closeness to
California is the obvious explanation for a market that mostly serves U.S. patients, but the city
tells you nothing about the quality of a particular clinic. Each one is verified the same way,
wherever it is.

### Is the cell therapy market in Mexico regulated?
Yes. COFEPRIS authorises each establishment by activity: applying treatments, storing cells or
collecting them. The licence covers the establishment, not whether a treatment works.

### Is it cheaper than in the U.S.?
Quotes are usually lower — but many therapies offered in Mexico are not legally
available in the U.S. outside a clinical trial, so there is not always an equivalent price to
compare against.

### Is Cancún on the list?
Yes. Quintana Roo has 6 entries, in Cancún and Cozumel, all from 2017 and 2018.
