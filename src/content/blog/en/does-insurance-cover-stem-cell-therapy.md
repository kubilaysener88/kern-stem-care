## Short answer

Plan on paying for it yourself. U.S. private insurers and Medicare generally treat mesenchymal stem cell
injections for joints, back pain or wellness as investigational, and decline them. Canadian provincial
plans cover care abroad only for services they already insure at home, and these therapies are not
among them. That holds just as much for a clinic in your own city as for one in Mexico.

There are a few things worth checking before you write the whole trip off as uninsurable: the tests
your own doctor orders at home, how a health-spending account treats the invoice, and whether a travel
policy covers complications. Each is covered below.

Regenerative cell therapies are considered investigational and are not approved by the U.S. FDA or
Health Canada for these uses. Results vary from person to person.

## Why insurers say no

Insurers pay for treatments that are approved or accepted as standard care for a specific condition.
Stem cell injections for arthritis, tendon injuries or general wellness have not reached that point.
The trials are small and the protocols differ, which is why insurers file them under "experimental or
investigational." That label is what the denial letter will cite.

You may have read that insurance *does* cover stem cell transplants. It does, and that is a different
procedure. A bone marrow or stem cell transplant for leukemia, lymphoma or myeloma rebuilds the blood
and immune system, and it is approved standard care. Regenerative injections into a knee or a vein are
not the same treatment, and the coverage for one tells you nothing about the other.

## What may still be covered — and what usually isn't

| Item | Usually covered? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The cell treatment itself | No | Plan for it as out of pocket |
| X-rays, MRI or blood work ordered by your own doctor at home | Often, as ordinary diagnostics | Ask your doctor; these are the studies the treating physician will want to see anyway |
| Flights, hotel and meals | No | Budget them separately — see what sits [outside our quote](/en/pricing/) |
| Treatment of a complication once you are home | Varies — some plans exclude complications of uncovered care | Ask your insurer this exact question, in writing, before you travel |
| Health-spending account (HSA, FSA) reimbursement | Uncertain — see below | Ask your plan administrator before you pay |
| A complication while you are still in Mexico | Only if a travel policy says so | Read the exclusions for planned medical procedures |

### Medicare

Original Medicare generally does not pay for care received outside the United States, and it does not
cover regenerative stem cell injections at home either. If you have a Medicare Advantage or Medigap
plan, ask about its foreign-travel benefit — those usually cover emergencies, not planned treatment.

### HSAs and FSAs

This is the question people most often hope has a yes. The honest answer is that it depends, and it is
not ours to give. The IRS defines qualifying expenses in Publication 502. That publication excludes
cosmetic procedures, so skin and facial treatments are unlikely to qualify. It also has a specific rule
about drugs used in another country, and a cell product that the FDA has not approved fits awkwardly
under it. Ask your plan administrator or tax preparer before you pay, not after.

### Canada

Provincial health plans generally pay for out-of-country care only when it is an insured service and
was approved in advance. Investigational cell therapies are not insured services. Whether any of the
cost can go toward the federal Medical Expense Tax Credit is a question for your accountant, who can
check the Canada Revenue Agency's rules against your invoice.

### Travel insurance

Many standard travel medical policies exclude treatment that is the reason for the trip, and some
exclude its complications too. Specialist medical-travel policies exist that cover complications of a
planned procedure. Whichever you look at, read the exclusions, not the brochure, and ask the insurer
directly whether a planned regenerative injection in Mexico is covered.

## FAQ

### Does Blue Cross Blue Shield cover stem cell therapy?
Not for regenerative uses such as knee or back injections, as a rule — like most large insurers, it
lists them as investigational. Policies vary by state and plan, so check your own plan's medical policy
or call the number on your card.

### Will my insurance cover stem cell therapy for knees?
Generally not. Knee injections are the most studied use, but the evidence is still short of what
insurers require for standard care. What the research does and does not show is in our post on
[knee cartilage](/en/blog/can-stem-cells-repair-knee-cartilage/).

### Can I get reimbursed later if I keep the receipts?
Possibly, but don't count on it. Submitting an itemized invoice costs nothing, and some patients do it.
Ask first whether the plan will even consider the claim, so the decision to travel doesn't rest on a
reimbursement that may never come.

### Will my insurer refuse to treat me afterwards because I had this done in Mexico?
Having had an uncovered treatment does not normally cancel the rest of your coverage. Some plans do
exclude complications that result from uncovered care. That is why it is worth asking your insurer
before you go, and getting the answer in writing.

### Is the free evaluation really free?
Yes. No deposit and no obligation. You pay only once the treating physician has confirmed you are a
candidate and you have the written quote.
